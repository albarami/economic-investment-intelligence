# Look proof of the living map's opening (S3-T15)

A development page that shows the opening of the living map as `docs/11_DESIGN_DIRECTION.md` describes it, on the 1920 x 1080 CSS canvas of the wall, in English and Arabic, in the dark and the light theme. It is the shell of tasks S3-T09 (Stage, Brief, Fact chip) and S3-T12 (the living map) pulled forward. It adds no product scope, and the production build of `web/` holds nothing of it.

Routes: `/en/_look` and `/ar/_look`. The query `?theme=dark` or `?theme=light` sets the theme; the top bar's control changes it in place. The page opens on the home; `?answer=operate` opens the answer to "Where should we operate?" directly (the captures and most browser tests use it).

## The home and its questions

The home starts from "What do you want to decide, and where?" over the skyline of West Bay, with four questions and no data layer, no legend and no numeral. "Where should we operate?" asks for a place (one place in this proof: Greater Doha and Lusail, the release's extent); choosing it flies out to the City framing, lights the layer class by class and lands the answer. The other three questions open a quiet "not in this proof" state with Back. Back from the answer puts the data out and returns to the skyline.

## What is on screen

- The Stage: the map of Qatar from an uncommitted PMTiles extract of the Protomaps daily build (OpenStreetMap data), styled in code by `@protomaps/basemaps` (the dark flavour in the dark theme, the light flavour in the light theme) with the page's tokens over it. Roads, coast and water are real. Every building footprint of the open buildings file (Overture Maps, uncommitted, built by `tools/build_buildings.py` into a PMTiles archive under `data/basemap/`; see `tools/README.md` for the height method) is drawn flat from zoom 13 and extruded from zoom 14 by its own height; below zoom 13 the basemap's buildings are drawn flat. From zoom 13 the roads step to street colours, cased in the light theme. Labels are the basemap's own, in the page's language, drawn through MapLibre's font faces as the glyph spike does: major places (localities the basemap draws from zoom 9 or lower) above every other name and never under the page's plates; district names from zoom 12, each name once: one label point per name inside Greater Doha and Lusail, chosen by the release build; main road names from 12, secondary streets from 14 and every named street from 15, along the line. The camera is the "City" framing of DES-60 (pitch 45, bearing minus 15), computed from the free part of the Stage so that no bright unit lies under the legend and no major place name (Doha, Al Rayyan and Lusail in the extract; the release carries their label points and names, read from the extract by `release-build.mjs`) touches a plate or the screen's edge: each stands wholly inside the screen, at least 8 px clear of it, or is not drawn. It stops at a city scale, zoom 12 at most (the token `--eii-city-max-zoom`, at or below the unfold zoom), so a lit layer gathered in one district still opens on the city with the Brief and the legend open.
- A limit of this proof: district, zone and street names may pass under the opaque plates. MapLibre cannot keep its symbols off the page's own plates, and only the major place names are kept clear, by the framing. The product's map may add collision obstacles under the plates later.
- One lit layer: firms per square kilometre by H3 release unit (resolution 9, Greater Doha and Lusail), a MapLibre fill on the five-step sequential ramp of section 4.3. Only the densest units are bright.
- The top bar: wordmark, the "Viewing as" badge with the seat's name, the theme control, Pause motion, the language control in the other language's script, Reset.
- The legend plate at the lower inline-start of the Stage: the scale from low to high, left to right in both languages (never mirrored), each swatch drawn as its class is drawn on the map (the fill at the class opacity over the land), the four breaks, a mark over the classes the card's share counts ("Densest areas", from the 300 break), the direction in words, the date of the register count ("As of 13 November 2026"), a key of the establishment dots and circles where the map shows them, and "Show as table".
- The Brief at the inline end with one card: the question as its eyebrow, one headline, up to three Fact chips, one caveat, "More evidence" (DES-140) and Back. While the layer is invented the headline names no place ("Firm density peaks in one compact core") and no district plate is drawn: a district is named only over a Public layer, and the release is refused otherwise. The mean figure is over the areas with firms, and its caption says so. An empty cell table shows “Not measured”, with no hero, mean, share or summary-method text and no “More evidence” control for an absent summary.
- At street zoom (from 13.5) the Brief and the legend fold to a slim strip; at rest below 13.5 they are open again. The folded Brief keeps the question, the headline and the caveat; the folded legend keeps its swatches, its breaks, its direction in words and its date, and "Show as table" works there too (while the camera moves they hold their state down to zoom 12). "More evidence" opens the source chain of the densest unit's figure, and each chip opens its own: the fact, its table, versioned method and producing pipeline. Origin stays in backend metadata; no provenance word or fixture label is rendered in the drawer or its accessible names (decision 0035 items 1, 4 and 5, supplied with the fix brief).
- The map attribution names OpenStreetMap contributors, Microsoft, the Overture Maps Foundation and Protomaps on one Latin line, and says in the page's language that building heights are estimates.
- Establishment points come from the register file. They cluster from zoom 12 to 14 (circles, no count) and are single points from zoom 14. Like the lit cells they are drawn under every label, so no point covers a place, district or road name. A point or circle is ink with a ring of the ground's colour, never a colour of the density ramp, and the legend keys it. A point opens a card of its published fields and the number of employees (the owner's decision of 2026-11-13); no wage and no other labour figure, and no coordinate rows. The employees and branches figures are Fact chips that open their source chain. "Highlight one type" is offered from zoom 14, where the single points are drawn: it lists the types under the heading "Establishments by type", each named from the catalogue with its count, and one picked type stays ink while the others dim (no hue per type: DES-21).
- An optional photoreal mode is shown only when the server has `MAPTILE_API_KEY`. The browser calls the page's own `/photoreal/` routes; the server adds the key. Before forwarding any root or tile request, the server durably reserves its charge in the project total under `data/photoreal/`, using an exclusive filesystem lock shared by server instances. Corrupt or unreadable accounting state, write or sync failures, and occupied or abandoned locks refuse forwarding; rejected or interrupted transports keep their reserved charge. A new accounting directory initializes a zero total durably under that lock. A missing state file in an existing accounting directory refuses the call. An abandoned lock or temporary write requires inspection; the server never steals or resets it. Tile response bytes are not stored. In that total, and any further forward is refused once that total reaches 3,333 (the owner's 20 dollar cap at 6 dollars per 1,000, free use ignored). The tileset screen-space error is 24, against the loader default of 8. Tile demand falls roughly with the square of that error, so one 1920×1080 view is expected to stay around 400 tiles. That figure is an estimate, not a measurement: a full-detail view at error 8 is taken as on the order of 3,600 requests, and 3,600 × (8/24)² = 400. The page stops requesting tiles once the session count passes 600 and shows the view as loaded. While the mode is on the open basemap and the building archive are hidden and the camera may pitch to 60 degrees (55 otherwise).

Every numeral on screen is a Fact chip. Every word comes from `catalogue.json`.

## The data behind the page

The page's server builds a release in memory when it starts (`release-build.mjs`). When `EII_REGISTER_FILE` is set it reads that register, with `EII_REGISTER_DATE` (YYYY-MM-DD, the collection date its README states; the release is refused without it); otherwise it reads the invented stand-in in `register/` (800 invented rows around twelve invented centres on land, with an invented `employees` count; see `register/README.md`). The lit layer is the count of those establishments per H3 cell of resolution 9 inside Greater Doha, shown as firms per square kilometre (the count divided by the cell area). Without a register file it still measures the extract's buildings and invents densities, and without either the page has no lit unit. Facts carry backend provenance independently: the stand-in and attached employee records are Synthetic; a configured register's own attributes, type counts and density are Public only with complete source metadata. Employee counts use a separate attached-labour table and never borrow the register publisher. Scale configuration has its own producing record. No figure is attached to a named place, building or business except the establishment card's permitted fields. Nothing derived from the extract is written to the repository. `release-shape.mjs` is the shape a release file of task S4-T11 can take in place of the invented one: per-unit aggregates, the facts with their chains, and named firms with name, activity, size band and unit only.

The source-chain contract is closed by `lineage.mjs`: each fact names its release table, method and method version, producing generator name and version, and input records with content hashes. Method and generator keys resolve to versioned implementation records. Generated inputs also carry `origin` and their generator; the release and generated layer carry backend origin and generator records. Missing links refuse the release and the Fact chip. The drawer shows neutral catalogue wording and method and pipeline versions through reference chips. More evidence opens the release build's input records: actual publisher, dataset, page, licence, retrieval date and hash for Public inputs, and dataset, producing pipeline/version and hash for generated inputs. Input records page within the drawer so the wall does not scroll (DES-36). A range chip offers both bounds' complete chains through explicit bound chips, one fact at a time (DES-95); Escape closes the drawer and returns focus to its opening chip. With no cells, no density summary or summary method is released or shown, and Why can't I see this? opens the coverage reason, rule and available scale alternative (DES-75, DES-134).

A configured register additionally requires `EII_REGISTER_SOURCE`, a JSON object with nonempty `publisher`, `dataset`, `source` (the source page), `licence`, calendar-date `retrieved`, and lowercase SHA-256 `sha256`. The hash must match the supplied file bytes. Missing metadata or a mismatched hash refuses the release; no fixture publisher is substituted. The building-derived density fallback also requires `EII_BASEMAP_SOURCE` with the same fields and a matching archive hash, and keeps both its Public geometry input and its Synthetic producing input. Fix round 6 read no real register or extract; round 7's tests use the existing local extract and tool installations, with invented register records only.

With a local extract present, the default browser run explicitly selects the committed invented register through `standInFile`; without the extract it selects the empty build root and supplies no stand-in. Both paths use an empty live key, isolated accounting and a rejecting upstream transport. A supplied `repositoryRoot` controls its own stand-in's presence: the empty root of the no-extract run supplies no register, so its release has no cells or summary facts. Ambient register and key settings, and caller-supplied transport overrides, are ignored in both runs. The browser-test child environment also clears the register settings and live key. The default run follows extract presence; the explicit no-extract run always uses its isolated empty root. Failed browser case names are preserved in the enclosing Node assertion even when child output is hidden. `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSERS_PATH` may identify an already installed headless shell; no test downloads it. Tests of a configured Public source use an invented file and invented attribution exclusively; they establish validation behavior, not the truth of a Public dataset.

Without the extract (the hosted job has none), the page stands on plain land with no lit unit, with unavailable density coverage when no register is supplied. Automated verification is recorded by fix round in `PROGRESS.md`; this page does not establish visual audit closure.

## Commands

From the repository root, with Node 22 first on `PATH`. The headless shell is the one the glyph spike installs (`npm --prefix web run spike:glyphs:install-browser`).

- `npm --prefix web run test:spike:look` runs the page's Node tests and its browser tests in the headless shell, once with the extract under `data/basemap/` and once against an empty folder without it.
- `npm --prefix web run spike:look:capture` builds and serves the page and writes `en-dark.png`, `en-light.png`, `ar-dark.png`, `ar-light.png`, `en-dark-greyscale.png`, `ar-dark-greyscale.png` and `en-dark-buildings.png` (zoom 15 over Lusail) at 1920 x 1080 into `.overnight/OUT/look/` at the repository root. It refuses when git does not ignore that folder, and it fails and names the missing file when there is no extract.
- The capture also writes the home in the four variants (`home-<lang>-<theme>.png`).
- `npm --prefix web run spike:look:capture:street` writes the sixteen street captures of V9 into `.overnight/OUT/look/street/` (`<camera>-<lang>-<theme>.png`): (a) 51.5369 E 25.2546 N, zoom 15.3, flat; (b) the same, zoom 15.8, pitch 55, bearing -20; (c) 51.527 E 25.322 N, zoom 14, flat; (d) 51.530 E 25.322 N, zoom 15.4, pitch 55, bearing -25, each on the answer screen through `window.__look.camera`.
- `npm --prefix web run spike:look:capture:3d` is for the lead alone, with `MAPTILE_API_KEY` in its environment. It asks the page's server for `/photoreal/status` and refuses (exit 1) when the mode is unavailable, before any browser or request to the imagery provider. Otherwise it opens one page in one session, turns the photoreal mode on, shows West Bay (51.530 E 25.322 N, zoom 16, pitch 60, bearing -25) and Lusail Marina (51.524 E 25.385 N, zoom 16, pitch 60, bearing 20), waits for each view's tiles (loaded, or held by the 600-tile budget, which the two views share), writes `.overnight/OUT/look/3d/westbay.png` and `lusail.png`, and prints the project counter before and after. No test runs it with a key.
- `npm --prefix web run spike:look:serve` builds the page and serves it on a loopback port; it prints the two addresses.

`./eii test` runs the page's Node tests, the browser suite included, in its `web-checks` suite, as `npm --prefix web run test:checks` does.
