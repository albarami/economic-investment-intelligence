## Context

- The pack banned every forecast: BRF-40 and BRF-49, rule 24 of AGENTS.md, the decline class `forecast` of the assistant and the Dropped list of [01_PROJECT_BRIEF.md](../01_PROJECT_BRIEF.md). It also dropped capture shares, revenue ranges, survival and entry rates, any number that combines presence with pay, and any extruded data. The reason given was that such figures, made from synthetic and simulated inputs, return the generator's own assumptions.
- On 2026-11-12 the owner decided that these limits came from assumptions that were wrong for this project: the project is to draw the most value from every dataset and to forecast wherever the data supports it, with every number computed by tested code and shown with its range, method and source.
- Rule 4 simulated all traffic. Rule 10, as [decision 0017](0017-public-data-additions-and-public-splits.md) left it, kept every Public split by sex away from any area the user chooses.
- The owner named six sources that need contact with no one, and put two keys for them into the uncommitted environment file.
- When the version-1 documentation merged, most line numbers that committed texts cited in the pack moved; the checks tested only that a cited line exists.

## Decision

### What stays

1. **The accuracy rules stay.** Every number is computed by tested code and never by a model (rules 16 to 18). No model-written prose reaches the screen, and every numeral is a Fact chip. No Synthetic or Simulated figure, and no estimate, share, score, rank or fit, is attached to a real named place, building or business (rules 8 and 24, PRV-65). Nothing gives a go/no-go verdict, advice to act, or a probability or confidence claim; rule 25 keeps those words. Nothing forecasts the success of a venture.

### Forecasts of published series

2. **Tested code forecasts published series.** `eii_core.calc` forecasts each series of a closed, versioned list in [07_ECONOMIC_METHODS.md](../07_ECONOMIC_METHODS.md). The first list is: licence issues and cancellations by activity and by the source's own area; property prices by district; hotel demand and room rates; consumer and producer prices; imports by product; announced investment by sector; and the construction pipeline, turned into expected new households and workers by municipality. A series is forecast only when the public data pack holds its source table; a named series without one is listed there as open. On screen the licence series keep their names, licence issues and licence cancellations (PUB-94), and no net series is formed from them.
3. **The model of a series is chosen by backtest.** The candidate models are a closed, versioned list. For each series the code runs a rolling-origin backtest and keeps the candidate with the smallest error by one fixed measure. No person and no language model chooses a model. A series too short for the backtest minimum gets no forecast: the answer is `NO_DATA` with a reason that says so, never a model without a backtest.
4. **Every forecast shows its interval and its measured backtest error.** The interval is empirical: it is built from the backtest's own errors at each horizon. Its caption states how many of the past values of the backtest fell inside the range that the other past errors gave, with both counts as Fact chips. Each past value is checked against a range that was built without its own error, so the count is measured and is not true by construction. It is never called a probability or a confidence level, and never said to hold for the next value. A forecast chart draws the interval as a band with that caption; the projection of card C21 keeps its own rules and stays a projection.
5. **A forecast is an Inferred fact.** Its subject and geography are those of its series. Its lineage names the method and version and the series; the model and the horizon identify the fact, and the interval ends, the backtest error and the number of backtest points are facts of the same computation, shown with it. Every forecast of card C30, of a Public series or of a released monthly series, is computed at request time by `eii_core.calc` from release tables, as a projection is (DAT-144); none is written to the release. It resolves to the source like any fact (rule 19). A forecast of a Public split by sex or nationality stays on the source's own geography and categories.
6. **The same code forecasts the released monthly statistics of the custodian-type feeds.** Over the released months of the window it forecasts pay level, earner count and wage bill, the stayer index at municipality level, and establishments and employment by area. Such a forecast is computed only from rows that are already released, so it reveals nothing that those rows do not. The tested code computes it in the product plane at request time, where only released rows exist, and it is never written to the release. It is a labelled projection from the released months, not a protected statistic of a later month, and PRV-54 is amended to say so: no protected statistic exists outside the window, and a series across windows is still refused. It carries the Synthetic class, the word "forecast" and the caveat that synthetic months show the format, not Qatar; its subject is the `area` of its series, and its origin is the last released month. A series is not forecast when a month of its window is carried forward, as card C21 already rules for projections. A short Public or released series answers that it has not enough history (item 3).

### Base rates

7. **Entry, closure and survival base rates come from the Public licence tables.** They are computed by activity and on the source's own geography, never for a drawn area. Each rate is shown with its numerator, its denominator and its period, and is computed only where the Public tables carry the denominator; elsewhere the answer is `NO_DATA` with its reason. Bulk months are handled by the stated rule of the method card. No figure of issues minus cancellations is shown (TST-54 stays). A base rate is worded as a share of past licences of that activity and area, never as the chance that the user's own venture survives; the word "odds" is never on screen. No such rate is computed from the Synthetic births and closures. A base rate is a Public fact with evidence status Observed, the status of the churn rate of card C2 (fixed arithmetic over published counts, caveat `calculated`), and it carries the caveat key `base_rate_not_your_chance`.

### The site estimate

8. **A site demand and revenue estimate.** For an area or pin that the user chose, a calibrated model of the outlet's catchment gives a low, a middle and a high revenue with its main drivers. Its inputs are the people present by time of day and pay band (item 11), the competitors from public listings as counts and distances, and category spend ranges calibrated to the Public survey of hotels and restaurants. It runs a seeded Monte Carlo over the stated ranges of its uncertain parameters; low, middle and high are fixed percentiles of the model's own draws and are worded as cases of the model; the main drivers come from a one-at-a-time swing computed in code. While any input is Simulated or Synthetic it carries the label "estimate on simulated inputs". Its subject is the `area`; it is never an estimate for a named real building or business, and no figure for a named competitor is computed for display, shown, stored or logged. A pay band is never called spending power: the spend per person by band is a stated, visible and editable range of the model. The estimate is off in public-only mode, and it is unavailable while the reconciliation of the restaurant totals (OI-03) keeps the survey figures off screen.

### Scenario tools

9. **A catchment-share scenario joins the break-even calculator.** Customers choose between the user's outlet and the real competitors from public listings, by distance and by an attractiveness that the user sets for the user's outlet against the competitors as a group, or by competitor category, never for one named business. The scenario shows the share of the user's outlet and the share of the competitors, split by competitor category and by distance band (for example Italian restaurants within 500 m; other restaurants), never per named business: a cell of that split that holds a single competitor is merged into the next wider cell. Where a single competitor is still left after those merges, the split is not shown, only the share of the user's outlet and the share of the competitors together; with one competitor in all, only the share of the user's outlet is shown and no share is stated for the competitors. A named competitor keeps only its Public facts and its distance. Every assumption is visible and editable. It is the user's scenario and is never called a forecast.
10. **A revenue range for the user's outlet.** The scenario tools show low, middle and high cases and the break-even point, from the Public survey of hotels and restaurants as the benchmark (revenue and costs per establishment, by activity), the simulated people present around the location at area grain, and the user's own assumptions (capture rate, average bill). The words "capture rate" name only that input of the user. The result is the user's scenario and is never called a forecast. The closed list of typed scenario inputs is extended for these inputs. Like the break-even calculator, it stays off screen while OI-03 is open. Measures of present size (a wage bill, retail sales, a number of firms) are measurements and stay allowed with their own class.

### People present by pay band

11. **People present by pay band through the day.** For a whole release unit, a day type and an hour, the product shows the simulated workers and residents present split by pay band. `eii_core.calc` computes it in the product plane as a fixed function of published rows only: the published presence groups of the unit's cells, and the published pay-band shares of the same unit (the worker-pay partition for workers present, the resident-pay partition for residents present). It is returned only when every row it draws on is published; a withheld, suppressed, carried-forward or remainder row is never filled in. Visitors have no pay band and are shown as one count without a band. The band shares are those of the unit's covered earners, so the figure does not separate people who have no wage record: it assumes that the workers and residents present have the pay bands of the unit's covered workers and residents, and its caveat says so. A count without a band is for visitors only. It is never apportioned to a drawn outline: both sides use the snapped unit list of PRV-18. It is Inferred and carries the Simulated mark, with the caveat that states that assumption. It is never called spending, spending power, income present or purchasing power.
12. **This is the one combination of presence and pay.** No table is added to the release and no dimension to the presence key (PRV-34 and PRV-58 are unchanged), and mobility still meets wage statistics only area to area (PRV-99). PRV-41 and MTH-77 gain one exception for the figure of item 11 and for the site estimate that reads it: a fixed function of published rows adds nothing to what those rows already release. Every other number that combines a protected value with a presence, flow or traffic value stays forbidden. The attack suite treats the figure as a function of observable release fields.

### Ranking

13. **A ranking may use them as criteria.** Forecasts, base rates and the site estimate may be criteria of the ranking of areas, each visible with its value and weight and removable by the user; the rank keeps its rank-stability sentence and shows which criteria moved it. A forecast or base rate of a Public series ranks only candidates on that series' own geography, or enters as an apportioned count where card C18 allows one. The site estimate, and any criterion with a Simulated or Synthetic input, is off in public-only mode. No Public split by sex or nationality is a criterion, and no list of firms is ranked by a protected or Simulated figure (PRV-125). The screen keeps the name "Areas ranked by the criteria you set"; the word "best" stays off screen.

### The 3D view

14. **An optional 3D view.** It extrudes whole release units by one protected area statistic, or by the simulated crowd density. It is off by default; every extruded value is also readable as a Fact chip in the flat view; marks, suppression and the snap rule are those of the flat view; a building keeps its own height and is never extruded by a data value; only traffic and crowd density animate.

### Models on the contracts of the future feeds

15. **Every model reads the release contracts.** The models of this record read the tables that the five custodian-type feeds fill (business register, employment, wages, residence, mobility), named by their contract names only. Synthetic and simulated data fill them now; nothing says that real records are used.

### Six self-serve sources and two keys

16. **Two keys may be used by the build.** `TOMTOM_API_KEY` and `EARTHDATA_API_KEY` may be used now, within their free allowances. They are outside the keys of [decision 0018](0018-priorities-and-amendments-to-decision-0015.md) item 12, which stay unused. Neither is printed, logged, committed or put into a work tree, a brief or a sandbox. No test, gate, command or the guided story needs either. `./eii build-public` never fails because `EARTHDATA_API_KEY` is absent: it continues and leaves the night-lights layer out. The open choice is only whether it prints a warning line that names the key and the layer left out, or says nothing. Exit 6 stays for an unavailable network only. Tests never call a live service: their fixtures are made by hand in the documented response format, and no response of a live service is committed.
17. **Traffic has a provider switch.** The setting `EII_TRAFFIC_PROVIDER` is `simulated` by default: the simulated congestion by hour, as before. With `tomtom`, the key present and the presenter's offline control not set, the API process fetches live flow tiles and traffic-aware drive-time areas from the provider and passes them on. The browser never holds the key and still contacts only its own origin. Nothing of a response is written to disk, to the release, to the ledger or to a log; the run-time lineage keeps the request, the retrieval time and the response hash. A live tile is a picture layer with the attribution "© TomTom" visible while the layer is on; no numeral is read from a tile. A traffic-aware drive-time area is an area of the session: it enters through the adapter, is bound to the session and seat like a drawn shape, and is marked with the provider and its retrieval time; figures over it follow the rules of a drawn shape and carry the least-real class of their inputs. The rules that traffic is computed only from released values, and that the same question returns the same numbers, hold for the default; with the provider on, a figure over a stored session area is repeatable for that area. Read on 2026-11-12: the free allowance is monthly (200,000 tile requests; 2,500 reachable-range requests); the developer terms page could not be read, so its rule on storing responses is not verified, and nothing is stored.
18. **The footfall simulation keeps its anchors and gains one.** The simulation stays the base, behind the mobility contract. The anchors of [decision 0017](0017-public-data-additions-and-public-splits.md) item 4 keep their roles. The new anchor is the hour-of-day shape of relative busyness, pooled over the named venues of each place category: it calibrates the hourly shape of simulated visits by category. No venue's own level or shape enters a generator, and no Simulated figure takes its level from a named place.
19. **Google popular times are shown as Public facts of named venues.** The relative busyness by hour of a named venue is a Public fact of a `place_public` subject, labelled "Google Maps popular times" with its retrieval date. It is a relative index, never a count of people, and it never shares a component with an estimated-people figure. The project's own page reader fetches it inside `./eii build-public`, for the named venues of a build-time list, never at run time; the values stay in the uncommitted public cache and never enter the repository or a test fixture; the source is left out of the nightly live fetch. The source states no licence for reuse, and the publisher's terms name automated reading (Sources). The owner decided on 2026-11-12 to use it in this personal internal project: this record names it under PUB-12, with `licence_basis: owner_decision`.
20. **Night-time lights.** NASA Black Marble VNP46A3 (monthly, on a 15 arc-second grid, about 500 m) is a Public layer of night-time radiance by area and month. It is fetched with the Earthdata token inside `./eii build-public` and aggregated to the frame by the public-data build. If `EARTHDATA_API_KEY` is absent, that command continues and leaves the night-lights layer out (item 16). NASA is acknowledged as the source and the product DOI is cited. It is worded as night-time radiance, not as a measure of business activity.
21. **Foursquare Open Source Places.** The dataset (Apache 2.0) is a further source of the places layer, for competitors and typical neighbours: its own table and attribution, a reviewed category mapping, and the real-name collision checks extended to it. It is fetched inside `./eii build-public` from the public copy on Source Cooperative, `https://data.source.coop/fused/fsq-os-places/`, which is geospatially partitioned GeoParquet and is read without an account; only the files that cover Qatar are fetched. Read on 2026-11-12: the copy's README states the Apache License, Version 2.0 and the publisher's copyright line; its listing holds three dated releases, the newest dated 2025-01-10, and states no update rhythm. The registry records the release used, and the places are shown with that date. The owner decided on 2026-11-12 that this copy, dated 2025-01-10, is used for version 1 as it is: no account is opened and no newer release is fetched for now. Where a place of the copy disagrees with OpenStreetMap or Overture, which are current, the newer source wins. In the de-duplication of the copy against the places layer (PUB-49, PUB-51) the record of the newer source is kept, and the copy's row for that place is not shown or counted against it; OpenStreetMap points are still not merged (PUB-55), and where the reviewer's cross-check against them disagrees with the copy, the reviewer follows OpenStreetMap.
22. **WorldPop population grids with age and sex.** The 100 m grids (CC BY 4.0) are Public population by age and sex, shown with their source and year. Whole cells of the published grid may be summed over an area the user chooses: a cell counts when its centre lies inside, and no cell is split. That sum is not an apportionment. It is display only: it is never a criterion of a ranking, never combined with a protected or Simulated figure, and never on one chart with one. The generator reads the grid only collapsed across age and sex, as weights; no synthetic record carries sex or age (SYN-13 and DAT-141 are unchanged).
23. **Open-Meteo hourly weather history.** The free interface (non-commercial use; data CC BY 4.0) is fetched by `./eii build-public` into the public cache as a hashed file, with the attribution the publisher asks for. The generator reads typical hourly profiles by season made from it, never a dated day; the constants of the modulation are manifest entries with ranges and a sweep; the method states how heat and the summer switch are kept from counting twice.
24. **Placement.** Each source is listed in [04_PUBLIC_DATA_PACK.md](../04_PUBLIC_DATA_PACK.md) with its publisher, licence or terms and retrieval date, and its work is placed in the public-data, simulation and map slices of [TASKS.md](../../TASKS.md).

### Rules of AGENTS.md

25. **Rules 4, 10, 24 and 25 read as follows** (CLAUDE.md is the same file byte for byte).
    - Rule 4: "Simulate mobility, footfall and geofencing locally. Never contact a telecom operator or use a real mobility feed; a published relative-busyness figure of a named venue is a Public fact, not such a feed. Simulate road traffic locally by default; the optional live traffic provider of decision 0019 is off by default, shows its flow tiles and traffic-aware drive-time areas with the provider's attribution, stores nothing of a response, and is never needed by a test, a gate, a command or the guided story."
    - Rule 10: "Add no nationality, sex or gender attribute, dimension or proxy on the synthetic or protected records or on anything derived from them. Public figures split by sex or nationality appear only as Public facts with source and year, in the source's own categories, and are never joined to a synthetic record, used to segment synthetic records or simulated presence, or combined with a protected figure. A tabulated figure stays on the source's own geography and is never apportioned to a drawn area; whole cells of a published population grid may be summed over an area."
    - Rule 24: "Never claim real custodian data, a Synthetic or Simulated fact about a real place, a Simulated or Synthetic footfall figure, or an estimate, share or score, attributed to a named building or business, certified or formal privacy, inferiority of an existing public service, ministry endorsement or operation, a forecast of the success of a venture, a forecast, estimate or base rate that tested code did not compute, or lookup of a firm, owner or individual."
    - Rule 25: "Give no go/no-go verdict, probability or confidence claim, and no advice to act. A ranking, fit or suggestion is shown only as the output of tested code over criteria the user can see and change, with its rank-stability sentence. A forecast is shown only with its interval from the backtest, its measured backtest error, its method and its source; an estimate with its low, middle and high cases, its main drivers and, while an input is Simulated or Synthetic, the label "estimate on simulated inputs"; a base rate with its counts and period; a user's scenario with every assumption visible and editable, never called a forecast. Show evidence, limitations and the cheapest next test."

### Citations

26. **The pack is cited by a stable anchor, never by line number.** A committed text cites a pack document by a requirement ID with its file, by a section heading (with the table row or the numbered item where one is meant), by a task ID, by a rule number or by the numbered item of a decision record. Every check that reads citations verifies that the anchor exists in the cited file. Rule 33 of AGENTS.md says so.

## What would change this decision

- A publisher's terms that forbid the use made here, found at a fetch, for any source but the one that item 19 names: the source is switched off and the owner is told (PUB-12).
- Hosting the demonstrator publicly or for a commercial purpose: the provider, the relative-busyness source and the free weather interface are off until the owner decides ([17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md](../17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md)).
- A further owner decision.
