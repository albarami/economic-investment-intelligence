## Context

- SYN-53 leaves the choice of the mechanism to slice S1: time-dependent speeds inside the routing engine, or speeds set on each edge. MTH-89 asks that α, β, the free-flow speeds, the capacities and the volume bands of card C25 are fixed at slice S1, before any traffic is built.
- Task S1-T03 routed in process on a synthetic road extract (ARC-88). Speeds set on each edge worked: the drive area changed with the speeds, and equal speeds gave equal areas. The engine's own time-dependent speeds gave different outlines for two hours with the same speeds; the cause is not confirmed.
- Task S1-T11 measured the installed engine (pyvalhalla 3.9.1) on synthetic extracts. It stores a link's speed as a whole number of kilometres per hour and drops a fraction: a posted 35.5 was stored as 35. It ignores a posted speed below 10 and takes the default of the road class: a posted 9 on a residential road was stored as 35. A posted 141 was stored as 140, and a posted 255 took the default of the road class. It measures an edge's length itself, from the coordinates of the extract, and reports it in whole metres: links declared as 10,000 m and 6,000 m measured 10,011 m and 6,007 m until the fixture's coordinates were corrected. It prints a route's time to the millisecond. It adds a cost at some junctions, which depends on the movement, and none of the costing options tried removed it (the default, a zero manoeuvre penalty, the shortest-distance costing, and that costing without hierarchy pruning gave the same delays). It draws a drive-time area as an outline from a grid.
- The first form of the spike computed an exact speed in the method, released the ratio of that exact speed, and rounded it to a whole speed only on the product side. An internal review of that form (four lenses, each with a second reader) showed that it gives a user two speeds or two times for one link and hour in four ways: a released ratio could imply a speed other than the one shown (0.294 of 35 km/h is 10.29 km/h, shown as 10); the card's time differed from the shown time by up to about 5%; the product timed a link by a length the engine does not use (6,000 m against the 6,007 m the engine measured), so every engine time was about 0.1% off the product's (0.117% on the 6 km links, 0.11% on the 10 km link, 0.099% on the hourly links); and the product handed out the engine's own route times beside its own.
- The owner decided on 2026-11-13: "a user must never see two different times or speeds for the same link and hour", and the limit is settled "by one rule that the product and the engine share": the released link speeds are floored at 10 km/h everywhere, the floor is stated on the method card and proved with a fixture, "so the exact arithmetic and the engine agree". A floor alone does not make them agree, because the engine stores whole speeds; this record therefore adds rules of the lead's own, and says so: the whole speed of item 2, the one length of item 4, the loading distance and the tie rules of item 5, the printed resolution, the rounding and the refusals of item 6, and the situation's whole speed of item 8 (OI-123 puts them to the owner).
- On the engine's turn costs the owner decided the same day: "keep the engine's turn costs, but itemise them instead of marking every time. A route's breakdown shows its link times plus one line, "turns at junctions +N s", so the parts add up to the total. Drive-time areas say once, on the legend and the method card, that they include turn costs at junctions. No product-side recomputation; slice S3 is unchanged."
- And on tolerances and the end of the spike: "A test bound must enforce exactly what the method card claims", and "After this pass, close the spike. Any further difference between the method and the engine is measured, pinned in a test and recorded on the method card as a stated limit, not another accuracy pass. Changes to the product because of those limits belong to the slice S3 tasks."

## Decision

1. **The mechanism of SYN-53 is a speed on each edge.** For an hour of a release, every link has one whole speed (item 2). Hours with the same whole speed on every link share one routing graph, built from the road extract of the release with each link's speed set to its whole speed. The engine's own time-dependent speeds are not used. Routing stays in process (ARC-88), and a walk area does not depend on the hour.
2. **One speed and one time for a link and an hour, made by the method.** For a link with assigned volume v above zero at hour h, the volume-delay rule gives the exact speed x = s0 / (1 + α (v / c)^β); the link's speed is w = max(s_min, round_half_up(x)), a whole number of km/h, and its time is L / w. A link without a row has the free-flow speed s0 of its class and the time L / s0. The released `speed_ratio` is w / s0 rounded half-up to three decimals; because s0 is a whole number of at most 140 km/h, round_half_up(speed_ratio × s0) gives w back exactly, and that is how the product reads it. The volume band still comes from the exact ratio of volume to capacity. w and L / w are the only speed and time of the link at that hour that anything shows or uses: the traffic layer, a route's link times, a drive-time area, the travel-time matrix and an access score by hour; the routing graph of the hour carries the same whole speeds.
3. **The speed floor.** s_min is a parameter of card C25, fixed with α and β; in version 1 it is 10 km/h, the lowest speed the routing engine accepts. The configuration check refuses a missing floor, a floor that is not a whole number or is below 10, and a road class whose free-flow speed is not a whole number, is below the floor or is above 140 km/h, the highest speed the engine stores. The product refuses a file of rows in which a ratio is not written with three decimals, is 0.000 or is above 1.000 (as outside the contract), or gives back a whole speed below the floor, or is not the ratio of that speed rounded half-up to three decimals (both naming the file and the link); it never adjusts a speed itself.
4. **One length.** The length L of a link is the length that the routing graph of the release holds for the link's edge, in whole metres as the engine reports it; a length that is not a whole number of metres is refused in both planes; `road_link.length_m` holds that length, and every time of item 2 is computed on it. The engine's own formula for a length, and the radius of the earth it uses, are not identified; a test compares every link with the engine's measure.
5. **Loading, paths and ties.** A release unit's trips start and end at the drivable node nearest its centroid by great-circle distance on a sphere of radius 6,371,008.8 m (the mean radius of the Geodetic Reference System 1980), ties to the lower node id, until the engine's own distance function is identified; the difference of latitudes is taken exactly before the distance is evaluated, so that two nodes at equal distance tie exactly. A distance on degrees is not used: near latitude 25 it chose another node than the distance on the ground. The free-flow shortest path of the assignment breaks a tie by the lexicographically smallest node sequence, then by the smallest sequence of link ids; identifiers are compared as text, by code point. A released flow between two units whose loading nodes have no directed path between them makes the assignment return `NO_DATA` (reason `no_path`), naming the pair.
6. **A route: its link times and one line for the turns at junctions.** A route's links are those of the routing engine's route over the routing graph of the hour. The product shows each link's time L / w and one line "turns at junctions +N s", where N is the engine's route time minus the exact sum of the link times: zero when that difference is within the engine's printed resolution of 0.001 s for each link of the route, otherwise rounded half-up to whole milliseconds. The route's total is the exact sum of its link times plus that line, so its parts add up exactly, and it differs from the engine's printed time by at most 0.001 s for each link. A route whose engine time lies below the sum of its link times by more than that resolution is refused, never shown, and so is a route of one link (it passes no junction) whose engine time differs from its link time by more than 0.001 s. The line holds everything the engine adds beyond the link times: on the fixtures that is turn costs only; on a real network the engine's costs at gates, tolls, ferries and border crossings would join it, and the slice S3 task that shows routes names or separates them. The spike routed from node to node only; how the end links of a route that starts or ends inside a link are timed (an area's pin, a gateway) is fixed in writing by the slice S3 task that shows such routes, and the rule that the parts add up applies to it unchanged. A route and an area by hour carry the caveat `traffic_simulated`. The product computes no route of its own.
7. **A drive-time area includes the turns at junctions.** A drive-time area is the engine's outline over the routing graph of the hour; it includes the engine's turn costs at junctions, and its legend and card C25 say so once. Because the engine draws the outline from a grid, its edge does not follow the route times exactly; card C25 states the measured limit (item 9).
8. **A situation.** For a dial situation, a link takes the whole speed round_half_up(L / m), where m is the arithmetic mean of the link's times L / w over the hours of the situation (a link without a row at an hour taking L / s0 for that hour), and the time L over that speed; the situation's routing graph carries those speeds. The speed is never below the floor, because no hourly time is longer than L / s_min. The situation fixture of MTH-90 becomes 53 km/h and 600/53 minutes (11.32 minutes to two decimals): the mean of 12.5 and 10 minutes on 10 km is 11.25 minutes, 53.33 km/h, which the engine cannot hold.
9. **The limits of the engine, measured and pinned.** Task S1-T11 measured the differences between the method and the engine on its three synthetic fixtures (the worked fixture of card C25, an hourly fixture and a crossing) and pinned each in a test whose tolerance is the claim that card C25 states:
   - a single link: the engine's printed time agrees with L / w within 0.001 s (largest difference measured: 51/59,000 s);
   - a route: the engine's printed time agrees with the route's itemised total within 0.001 s for each link of the route (largest junction line on the 144 routes of the hourly fixture's day and the 6 of the worked fixture: 0.018 s);
   - turns at junctions depend on the movement: at the crossing, 0.017 s straight through, 2.105 s for one turn and 5.394 s for the opposite turn; 0.017 s straight through the middle node of the hourly fixture, where two two-way links meet; nothing at the middle node of the worked fixture. Each is pinned within 0.001 s, so a changed engine calls for requalification;
   - a drive-time area: at a turn, a node's entry into the outline moves by the difference of the two route times (the turn's cost less the straight-through cost) within 0.2 s at every approach of the crossing and at both of its speeds (largest measured: 0.151661 s, on 2026-11-13), which shows that the outline includes turn costs; and a node of the fixtures enters the outline up to 40 s from its route time (largest measured: 34.004 s, on 2026-11-13; the bound adds a stated margin);
   - a node's entry into an outline is found by bisection to within 0.01 s; the search stops at twice the sum of the hour's link times or at 120 minutes (a request limit of the engine), whichever is smaller, and is not a tolerance; nine of the 27 measurements on the worked fixture concern nodes that cannot be reached from the pin, which have no route time and no entry up to that limit (44, 49 and 120 minutes at free flow, hour 8 and hour 9);
   - lengths: every link of the fixtures measures exactly its declared length in whole metres; the spherical distance of item 5 agrees with an independent, well-conditioned formula within 0.0000001 m at loading distances, a check of floating-point evaluation only;
   - on the worked and the hourly fixtures (not on the crossing, whose turn costs can lead the engine to another route), for every ordered pair of nodes that has a path, the engine's route uses the links of the method's shortest path over the link times, and their times sum exactly to the method's shortest time.

   These are measurements on synthetic fixtures, between their nodes; none of them gives the size of these differences on the real road network, and the last one is not known to hold there, where turn costs may lead the engine to another route. A further difference is measured, pinned and stated on the card in the same way, never answered by another accuracy pass of the spike; a change to the product because of these limits belongs to the slice S3 tasks.
10. **The version-1 values of card C25** (MTH-89). They are fixed here, before any traffic is built, are written in the release's method note, and are never tuned to a published figure or to a provider's tile (MTH-04).

    | Value | Version 1 | Basis |
    |---|---|---|
    | α and β | 0.15 and 4 | The values commonly used with the volume-delay form of the Bureau of Public Roads; as published |
    | Free-flow speed by road class (motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary, residential) | 105, 90, 75, 60, 50 and 35 km/h | The routing engine's own default speed for each class, so that a free-flow drive time and the assignment use the same speed; a project choice on a published basis; ASSUMPTION: not measured for Qatar |
    | Capacity by road class, in vehicle trips an hour for one direction (same order) | 4,000, 2,000, 1,500, 1,000, 600 and 600 | The default lanes times the default capacity of a lane in the network reader of an open-source transport simulation; ASSUMPTION: not measured for Qatar, and one capacity for a class because a road link of the data contract has no lane count |
    | Volume bands | four bands, `vb1` to `vb4`, with edges at 0.85, 0.95 and 1.00 of capacity | Thresholds that the Federal Highway Administration publishes for signalised intersections, used here as display bands for road links; ASSUMPTION |
    | Speed floor | 10 km/h | Project rule by the owner's decision of 2026-11-13: the lowest speed the routing engine accepts (item 3) |

11. **No mark on each time.** A shown speed or time never comes from a second calculation: the traffic layer, a route and an area use the whole speeds and lengths of items 2 and 4, a route's parts add up to its total, and an area says once on its legend that it includes turn costs. The limits of item 9, the floor and the whole speeds are stated on card C25.

## Consequences

- Card C25 of [07_ECONOMIC_METHODS.md](../07_ECONOMIC_METHODS.md) states the whole speed, the floor, the length, the loading distance, the itemised route, the situation rule and the limits of item 9 in its formula and its limits, and holds the floor fixture; MTH-89 lists the floor with the other fixed values; the situation fixture of MTH-90 becomes 53 km/h and 600/53 minutes. Document 03 makes `road_link.length_m` a whole number of metres, the routing graph's length, describes `speed_ratio` as the ratio of the whole speed, adds the floor to DAT-139, and has DAT-153 hold the itemised route totals of item 6 over the whole speeds. SYN-53 of [05_SYNTHETIC_WORLD_AND_SIMULATION.md](../05_SYNTHETIC_WORLD_AND_SIMULATION.md) names this record.
- The spike's code is not the product's. The task that builds the traffic method puts the whole-speed rule, the situation rule and the loading distance into `eii_core.calc` as tested functions used by both planes, and the release build writes `road_link.length_m` with the routing graph's own measure and the travel-time matrix with the itemised route totals of item 6 on the same routing graphs, so that its times include the same turn costs and equal the totals a route shows.
- The slice S3 tasks that show routes and drive-time areas carry the itemised route, its caveat and the legend's sentence on turn costs, fix in writing how a route from a point inside a link is timed and how the line of turns names costs other than turns, and decide what the product does about the limits of item 9; the product does not recompute a route or an area.
- A release has one routing graph for each distinct set of whole speeds among its hours, day types and summer settings, and one for each situation. How many that is on the real road network, and what building them costs, is not measured.
- A change of any value of item 10 after a freeze is a change of the method and needs a new build (MTH-89; rule 12 of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) for the release it enters).

## What would change this decision

- A version of the routing engine that stores speeds more finely, accepts speeds below 10 km/h, prints times more finely, measures lengths otherwise or changes its turn costs: the floor, the whole-speed rule, the length rule and the pinned limits are then measured again together.
- An explanation of the different outlines that the engine's own time-dependent speeds gave for equal speeds, with a time-zone database as part of the build: the mechanism may then be chosen again.
- Capacities or free-flow speeds published for Qatar's roads under a licence the pack admits: they replace the assumed values through a new record.
- A measurement on the real road network that shows too many routing graphs for a release to build in reasonable time, or routes whose links differ from the method's shortest paths.

## Open questions and assumptions

- OI-120 holds the mechanism as an assumption for the owner; OI-123 holds the values of item 10 (the free-flow speeds, the capacities and the number and edges of the volume bands are assumptions), the lead's rules that this record adds to the owner's floor (items 2, 4, 5, 6 and 8), and the changed situation fixture of MTH-90.
- Document 03 states the unit of `flow_hourly` (vehicle trips in the hour, DAT-139). This record does not decide how the mobility simulation of document 05 produces vehicle trips; a release whose flows are not vehicle trips per hour returns `NO_DATA` (MTH-88).
