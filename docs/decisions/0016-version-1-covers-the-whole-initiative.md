## Context

- [Decision 0013](0013-one-deep-journey-before-breadth.md) limited version 1 to one deep journey with three playbooks and put breadth in an ordered backlog, L1 to L10. The Dropped list of [01_PROJECT_BRIEF.md](../01_PROJECT_BRIEF.md) section 9.3 excluded free-drawn areas, 24-hour animation, named synthetic partner, supplier or prospect lists and road assignment. Presence was a unitless index, "never a count" (BRF-02, PRV-60), and every area came from a pin snapped to the fixed grid ([decision 0006](0006-fixed-grid-and-pin-based-areas.md)).
- On 2026-11-12 the owner decided that geofencing, footfall and traffic are headline features, and then that version 1 covers the whole initiative and must offer more than every service that already exists for companies looking at Qatar. That second decision includes and extends the first. Slice S0 continues unaffected.
- The capability bar is set by what existing services offer: commercial geography with sector patterns, best-location search and licence statistics by region and activity; footfall, crowd density, time patterns and event analytics; crowd and transport monitoring around large events; labour-market analysis with reporting and projections; investor introductions, partner search, investment opportunities and public tenders; and the habits that trusted statistical and commercial tools share (draw-an-area profiles, a source tile on every number, drive-time and walk-time trade areas, a home and work toggle, comparison with the company's best site, fixed auditable analyses behind an assistant, one-page exports, protected cells marked rather than left blank). The pack describes these services by what they offer and never names them ([decision 0012](0012-neutral-source-labels-and-no-product-comparisons.md)).

## Decision

### What version 1 delivers

1. **One living map of Qatar.** Real public geography, roads and business points; synthetic firms and workers as density and statistics at realistic levels, calibrated to public licence and census counts; simulated presence, crowd density and traffic through the day. Every Synthetic or Simulated figure carries its mark.
2. **Seven decision journeys, each ending with its own answer.**

   | No. | Company decision | The answer the journey ends with |
   |---|---|---|
   | D1 | Whether to enter Qatar | The market entry pack: market profile, customers and competitors, local income context, candidate areas, workforce context and next actions |
   | D2 | Which location fits | The location choice for a drawn or travel-time area, with the best-location ranking and its reverse (the flagship) |
   | D3 | Where to find customers | The customer finder: a targeted territory and a prospect list of named synthetic firms by sector and size band |
   | D4 | Whom to partner with | The partner and supplier finder: named synthetic firms ranked by fit, real businesses from public listings, public tenders where a public source exists, and a structured qualification route |
   | D5 | How to organise staffing | Staffing and pay: pay by occupation with middle ranges and earner counts (Synthetic), where such workers live, trends, national mean pay and wage trends by economic activity (Public, item 9), and the labour-market view |
   | D6 | Where to grow in Qatar | Where to grow: openings and closures over time, cluster change and a second-site comparison (shared reach and overlap, never lost sales) |
   | D7 | How to serve the region | Serving the region: travel times from an area to the ports, the airport, the free zones and the land routes, complementary firms, and the import context |

3. **Three services as journey outputs.** S1, the market entry evidence pack, turns interest into a specific, testable operating proposal. S2 compares growth and operating options (a new branch, a larger site, a new territory, a revised service model) with the barriers to resolve. S3 connects a company with local supplier candidates (named synthetic firms and real businesses from public listings) through a structured qualification route, with aggregate workforce evidence for procurement, recruitment and training.
4. **Four cases as guided stories**, each with a one-click start: C1, an industrial services firm asking whether there is enough industrial demand for a permanent local operation; C2, a technology supplier choosing which sectors and firms to approach first; C3, a distributor weighing a second depot, a larger current site or a local partner, with a synthetic file of its own delivery records; C4, a retail chain asking whether a weak branch suffers from its area or from itself, with a synthetic file of its own branch sales. The cases are illustrative. The sample files are marked Synthetic, stand for the company's own records, and stay inside the session and seat that loaded them.
5. **Best location and its reverse.** Candidate areas for an activity are ranked with every criterion, its weight and its value visible; the user can change the weights; a rank-stability sentence stands beside every rank; a public-only mode exists. The reverse lists which activities fit a site, for property owners matching tenants to the business mix and the catchment's wage profile. Similar-areas search uses three area types. Reachable access is a travel-time catchment score, by hour where congestion changes travel time (Simulated). A ranking, a fit or a suggestion is the output of tested code over criteria the user can see and change; it is never a verdict, a probability, a confidence claim or a revenue, demand or success forecast, and it is never worded as advice to act.
6. **Geofence.** The user draws an area, sets a radius, or picks a walk or drive time from any point, by hour of day. Public point layers and Simulated layers are computed for that exact shape. A public statistic published on an official geography is shown on that geography; where it is apportioned to the shape, the result is labelled Inferred and names its method and its geography. Only figures from the protected record types (business register, employment, residence, wages) snap to whole release units, with the snap sentence the pack already has, before any figure is read.
7. **Footfall and crowd density.** Simulated people present inside the area through the day, hour by hour, for every dial situation and the summer switch and for three event days (National Day, a day in Ramadan, a major match), shown as estimated counts with a range and split into workers, residents and visitors; crowd density (people per square kilometre) is drawn on the map. The wording is "estimated people around this location", never "footfall of" a named building or business. The custodian plane releases these counts only at release-unit grain under the disclosure controls of [06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md](../06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md) (panel minimum, complementary suppression, rounding); the product plane apportions released counts to the drawn shape, which is post-processing of released values. Their seasonal shape is calibrated and checked against public visitor counts ([decision 0017](0017-public-data-additions-and-public-splits.md)). This replaces "an index, never a count".
8. **Traffic.** Simulated congestion by hour on the real road network, drawn on the map and animated through the day; drive-time areas change with the hour. The simplest form is built: hourly origin-destination flows, as released, are assigned to the road graph by tested code with one fixed volume-delay rule. Traffic is Simulated and never computed from protected rows.
9. **Income geography with trends.** The six area-level indicators for covered earners: average and median wage by residential area where groups are large enough; wage bands and their spread; the covered wage bill and the earner count; residential income set beside presence by time of day; residence and workplace geography (broad commuting catchments and access gaps, where workplaces are reliably known); and wage trends by sector and area, separated from changes in worker mix. The average is never shown alone. The figures describe covered earners and wages paid, not every household, wealth or spending power. By the owner's note of 2026-11-12, the two stayer wage bills behind the wage trend of the same earners are ordinary wage-bill leaves, treated like any other leaf and never released, and the trend is released only at municipality level or coarser, with the draws that stayer bills of nested units share stated as a limit of the audit ([06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md](../06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md) PRV-55 and section 17; [17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md](../17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md) DHA-25). [Decision 0007](0007-disclosure-control-by-pooling-and-stable-treatment.md) item 2 is amended by the owner's answer of 2026-11-12 (item 22): the `money` and `quartile` pay partitions of resident and workplace pay gain one municipality level inside the same laminar family, a third cross-root union, with PRV-13 and PRV-16 amended for it, so that pay figures and the trend, still released only above its stayer threshold, are shown per municipality, and wage trends by sector come from the public portal's wage series by economic activity, never from a protected split, national by sector being the finest sector view in version 1.
10. **Monthly periods.** The release holds several monthly synthetic periods, so that "what changed" and wage trends work. They sit inside one protected window: the window's protected `period` becomes the span of its months, and the `window_key` formula, one `window_key` per `world_seed` and the freeze rules of AGENTS.md rule 12 are unchanged. Each month passes the same disclosure controls as a single period, and one treatment holds for every month of the window ([decision 0007](0007-disclosure-control-by-pooling-and-stable-treatment.md)). The attack suite gains differencing attacks across months, which must recover nothing with the controls on; no privacy invariant is weakened. Defences that a deployment on real records needs beyond these (release cadence, rolling windows, query auditing) are listed in `docs/17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md`.
11. **Openings, closures and the labour market.** Openings and closures over time on the map: public licence issues and cancellations on their official geography (Public) and synthetic firm births and closures by area and month (Synthetic). A labour-market view of sectors, occupations, pay and trends, with simple projections from tested code over published or released series, each marked as a projection with its method and period; a projection is never a revenue, demand or success forecast.
12. **Named synthetic firms.** Synthetic firms may be named, ranked and suggested as prospects, partners or suppliers. They are always marked Synthetic; their names never resemble real ones (the build fails on a collision with a real place or business name, BRF-31); they are never drawn as named points at a real address. A synthetic firm's record shows only its fictional name, activity, size band and area; its own wages, wage bill and headcount are never shown, and lists obey the same minimum, dominance and complement rules as any protected figure. Networks of firms or owners stay dropped.
13. **Real businesses.** Real businesses appear only from public places data and public listings, with public tenders where a public source exists, and the investment projects and sources of [decision 0017](0017-public-data-additions-and-public-splits.md). No Synthetic or Simulated figure is attached to a real named business, building or place.
14. **The assistant across every module**, in Arabic and English: it chooses the area and the indicators, calls fixed tested analyses and returns a map, a chart and a short brief with sources and caveats; a gallery of the four cases as one-click stories; a one-page brief export. It never types a number ([decision 0008](0008-assistant-never-types-a-number.md)); its closed catalogues grow to cover the journeys.
15. **Build order.** The map and data foundation first, then the flagship (the location choice) in full depth, then the other journeys. [14_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md](../14_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) and [TASKS.md](../../TASKS.md) carry the slices. Slice S0 is unchanged.

### What this replaces

16. [Decision 0013](0013-one-deep-journey-before-breadth.md) is superseded. Version 1 builds every journey above, in the order of item 15; the tool, playbook and stage-directive lists stay closed and versioned but grow to cover the journeys; the cut order of 0013 is replaced by the build order and the slice gates.
17. [Decision 0006](0006-fixed-grid-and-pin-based-areas.md) is amended. Its items 3 and 4 (areas only from a pin; every area snapped; no free drawing) now apply only to figures from the protected record types. The fixed grid stays the release grid of protected and simulated statistics.
18. The Later items L1 (ranking), L2 (reachable access), L3 (imports against local production), L4 (as monthly periods inside one window, item 10), L8 (the four cases) and L10 (similar areas) move into version 1. L5 (Neo4j loader), L6 (tool server), L7 (sovereign-mode trial) and L9 (product-space context line) stay Later.
19. These Dropped items return: named synthetic partner, supplier and prospect lists (item 12); road assignment (item 8); free-drawn areas (item 6); 24-hour animation, for traffic and crowd density only and always marked Simulated. Presence counts replace the presence index (item 7). Networks of firms or owners, invented attributes of real named places or businesses, and formal differential-privacy claims stay dropped.
20. AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md are amended in the same change: rule 8 names the subjects (an `area` accepts Public, Synthetic, Simulated and Inferred slots; `place_public` and the Public subjects of decision 0017 accept Public slots only; a named synthetic firm accepts Synthetic slots only and is never placed at a real address); rule 24 forbids a Simulated or Synthetic footfall figure attributed to a named building or business, in place of "named-building footfall"; rule 25 keeps "no go/no-go verdict, probability or confidence claim" and adds that a ranking, fit or suggestion is shown only as the output of tested code over visible criteria with its rank-stability sentence, never as advice to act.

21. Open items (owner's note of 2026-11-12): OI-07 is closed by item 12; OI-27 is closed: serving the region (D7) is in version 1, and a cross-country entry comparison stays out. OI-11 (real privacy thresholds), OI-26 (the deployment platform), OI-36 (layout signals before a real-data pilot) and the pilot part of OI-24 (an independent human attack before a real-data pilot) move to `docs/17_DEPLOYMENT_HARDENING.md` as real-deployment items. Every other open item stays; where one waits for the owner, its documented default applies.
22. [Decision 0007](0007-disclosure-control-by-pooling-and-stable-treatment.md) is amended. Its item 2 now lists three cross-root unions: the municipality remainder, the terminal `ha_home` unit and, by the owner's answer of 2026-11-12 (item 9), the municipality unit of `rp_all_money`, `rp_all_quartile`, `wp_all_money` and `wp_all_quartile`. That unit lies above its partition's units in the same laminar family and holds only exact sums of its published units' treated leaves, apart from the unreleased stayer bills of PRV-55 (PRV-13, PRV-16).

## What would change this decision

- A further owner decision on scope or on the order of the slices.
- A privacy attack that recovers a protected value through drawn shapes, months or firm lists: the release rule is tightened, never the attack ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) rule 12).

## Open questions and assumptions

- ASSUMPTION: the monthly periods are the months of the synthetic reference period (24 months, SYN-12); the number shown first is fixed at the slice S1 gate.
- ASSUMPTION: the three event days are simulated day types (National Day, a day in Ramadan, a major match at a stadium), each with its own hour-by-hour profile; their calibration sources are named in document 05.
- OPEN: the public source of tenders is confirmed at slice S2; without one, tenders are not shown.
- OPEN: the apportioning weight for drawn shapes (area, or a licensed public population grid) is fixed in document 07 and confirmed at slice S2.
