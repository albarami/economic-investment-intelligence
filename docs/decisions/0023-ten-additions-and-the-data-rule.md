## Context

- The owner asked which further tools would add the most value and approved six additions and four more "if time allows". Each is free and self-serve, needs no paid service and no key, and runs on public data or on the merged records of [decision 0022](0022-real-register-companies-with-a-synthetic-world.md) under the release rules of [06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md](../06_PRIVACY_AND_DISCLOSURE_CONTROL.md). By a note of the same evening the owner added a seventh, satellite imagery and address search, through a free-tier key on an account with pay-as-you-go disabled.
- The same evening the owner set a data rule: only data the project is sure to have or get is used. The lead audited every planned synthetic, simulated or invented source of the pack against that rule; six planned synthetic sources fell outside it, and three planned attributes went beyond what their source plausibly holds.
- Later the same evening the owner set binding rules for every talent answer and decided that the product is for companies only, designed for its three personas; his own notes of the same day allow one paid service, the photorealistic 3D mode, capped at USD 20 in total. On 2026-11-14 the lead ruled on the three findings of the review of this decision's sweep that needed a decision (ruling L-160), strengthening one privacy rule and weakening none.

## Decision

1. **A1 — Talent available near this site** (new task S6-T20). The answer is called "Talent available near this site", never "who can work for you", so that it never reads as a list of people (the owner's requirement of 2026-11-13, about 23:03). For a site, it shows two released figures side by side within 10, 20 and 30 minutes' drive, and a third: the talent pool (residents by occupation and pay band within reach), the competing demand (jobs of the same occupations within reach), and qualified people within reach per competing job within reach, by occupation group, at the same cut-offs. The third figure is the quotient of the card's own two released figures for the same occupation group and the same cut-off, with no further competition model in version 1. Its label says what it is: "qualified people per competing job within N minutes". It is shown to one decimal. The two-step floating catchment is not used for it. It is computed only from the two released tables, after the privacy engine's work (snapped release units, the minimum counts, noise and rounding), never from records and never from an unreleased value. It is post-processing of figures already shown and reveals nothing they do not. It is the only exception to PRV-30 and PRV-18. PRV-127 is a guard: it refuses any other ratio of two protected families and any ratio computed before release. Where either input is suppressed or below the minimum count, the ratio is not shown and the card says why (reason key `ratio_input_withheld`). Where the competing-jobs figure is zero, no ratio is shown and the card says that no competing job is within reach (reason key `no_competing_jobs`). Tested code in `eii_core.calc` computes the ratio (AGENTS rules 16 and 17), a Fact chip shows it, and its lineage names both released tables. Each catchment snaps to release units like every area (PRV-18). Home-to-work flows are drawn as a flow map (flowmap.gl) and animated as aggregate flows (deck.gl's TripsLayer), never as trails of individuals (SYN-59). It runs on the merged labour and residence records of decision 0022, released under document 06; every figure comes from tested code (AGENTS rules 16 and 17). Journey D5, the map and the guided story. These cases bind it, its home-to-work flows and every other talent answer, among them pay benchmarks and the Qatarization planner of item 15:
    - group statistics only, through the release rules: minimum counts of people and of employers in every cell, the dominance rule, and pay as bands, median and middle range;
    - never a name, an identifier, a residential address, one person's pay or a person-level row;
    - home locations no finer than the district: no building, street or block for where people live;
    - commute lines only as totals between district pairs that pass the minimums;
    - no employer named beside a talent pool or flow: a pool says how many people in an occupation live within reach, never where they work by name;
    - Qatari and non-Qatari splits only where the cell passes; otherwise the split is withheld with its reason.

    Four planted violations fail: a pool cell below the minimum, a district-pair flow below the minimum, an employer named beside a pool, and a home location finer than the district. Three further cases stand beside them: a talent ratio computed from records is refused (TST-257), a talent ratio with a suppressed input is refused (TST-258), and the talent ratio from the two released tables passes (TST-259). The owner answered on 2026-11-14: the third figure above is computed. Version 1 meets the case "commute lines only as totals between district pairs that pass the minimums" by drawing each line from one whole home municipality of `ha_home` to the site's catchment snapped on `ha_work_count`, an exact sum of published `home_area_share` rows, so that each end is an area, the home end is never finer than a municipality, and every total passes the minimums (card C39; OI-147).

2. **A2 — All of Qatar in 3D, with a clock** (new tasks S2-T20 for the heights and S3-T16 for the view). Building heights in this order: the recorded height; the floor count times a stated storey height; the open 3D building heights of 3D-GloBFP (CC BY 4.0; credit "Che et al. 2024"); the WSF 3D 90 m cell mean. 3D-GloBFP under-reads towers, so a recorded height always wins. The error of each fallback is measured against the recorded heights and shown behind "More evidence"; heights stay estimates (FE-52). A building-height source under a non-commercial licence is not used (the GlobalBuildingAtlas is CC BY-NC and is excluded). The clock: sunlight (deck.gl's LightingEffect with its sun light) with shadows, which deck.gl marks experimental, kept to one focus district; the map's light and sky; night lights at dusk (NASA's Black Marble); a time scrubber; and a before-and-after swipe for scenarios.

3. **A3 — The national GIS centre's 2026 layers** (new task S2-T19). From its public REST map service (`services.gisqatar.org.qa`), with no sign-in: land pricing by district (`LandPricing2026`: residential, city-centre residential, flats, villas and commercial prices), rail lines with planned stations and protection zones (`QRAIL_NO_OPERATION`), health centres and their catchments (`PHCC_LOCATION_2026`, `PHCC_CATCHMENT_2026_Q`), landmarks in Arabic and English (`Landmarks`), and the address locator (`QARS_Custom_Locator`). No licence text is published; they are public data under the owner's rule, cited with publisher, service, retrieval date and hash (AGENTS rule 5). They answer what land costs in an area and how near a planned station is: the cost view of journeys D2 and D5, and the map. The host joins the fetcher's allow-list in S2-T19. Widened by the owner ("anything you can take from there, take it, it's public"): the other public layers that the centre's public geoportal loads, each answering queries with no sign-in: land use and zoning (`Zoning`: permitted uses, height and setbacks, for site fit and as a ranking criterion), the official address points (`QARS`: zone, street and building numbers, the parcel number, the building subtype, the survey date and the point only; the utility account numbers and internal editing fields are never taken, as they identify households or add nothing), the approved land parcels (`CadastrePlots`: parcel number, area and dates), the commercial points (`POIs_COMM`), the official district and zone boundaries, and, as useful, protected areas, geographic names and road flow lines; and the imagery history (satellite 2003 to 2024, aerial 1995 to 2022), read through the service's export operation in web Mercator for a few fixed before-and-after views of how a place changed (journey D6); the satellite base stays that of item 7. Every derived file stays under `data/` and is never committed.

4. **A4 — Ask aloud and hear the answer** (new task S8-T10). Speech to text (Jais-ASR, Apache-2.0, which lists the Qatari dialect) and a voice (VoxCPM2, Apache-2.0, with Arabic) run on the workstation, in their own environment (the speech model pins a library version of its own); no audio leaves the machine. Questions may be asked in Qatari Arabic or in English. The transcript is shown and confirmed before the question runs. The voice speaks only template-writer text (AGENTS rule 21), never a number a model typed; in Arabic it speaks a one-line headline and the full answer stays on screen. The frame rate of the map is measured with both models loaded.

5. **A5 — Best three sites, the effect on a company's own branches, and "how sure"** (new task S6-T21). Location-allocation (maximal coverage, p-median, set cover) with PySAL `spopt` (BSD-3); Monte Carlo and Sobol runs with SALib (MIT) over the distance exponents, rents, wages and catchment limits, reported as "holds in X of 1,000 runs; most sensitive to ...". It extends card C34, the rank-stability sentence and the `branch_check` playbook; it gives no go/no-go verdict and no probability claim (AGENTS rule 25). The rents in these runs follow item 12.

6. **A6 — The financial centre's firms** (new task S3-T17). The financial centre's register ([decision 0022](0022-real-register-companies-with-a-synthetic-world.md)) as a map layer beside the commerce register's companies, with firms counted per tower; the geocoded file is delivered outside the repository and read through its setting (decision 0022 item 1).

7. **A7 — Satellite imagery, address search and the photorealistic 3D mode** (the look's mode switch, task S3-T15 or its successor; the assistant's place resolution). The owner's note of 2026-11-13: the product may read `ESRI_API_KEY` for the imagery provider's satellite basemap and geocoding within the free tier; pay-as-you-go stays disabled, so no spend is possible. A satellite mode on the open map shows the provider's world imagery under the product's own layers, with the attribution the provider's imagery style carries; online only, no tile is stored. Live address search in the ask bar and the place picker shows a result and flies to it; a result is never written to a file, the database or a log (geocoding not stored). The key is never printed, logged or committed and never reaches a worker; tests and continuous integration never call the provider, and a planted test fails on any request to the provider's hosts without the key. The map modes are then three: the open map, satellite and photorealistic 3D.

    The photorealistic 3D mode shows Google's photorealistic 3D tiles through its Map Tiles API, by the owner's own notes of 2026-11-13; it is the one paid service of this decision (item 14). Its key, `MAPTILE_API_KEY`, is read at run time only by the product's own server, which adds it to each tile request on a same-origin route; the browser never holds the key, which is never printed, logged or committed and never reaches a worker. The owner caps the paid spend at USD 20 in total: every billable tile request is counted project-wide, free use is ignored, and requests are refused once the count reaches 3,333 (USD 6.00 per 1,000 requests at the first paid tier). One view loads at most 600 tiles. The mode is never mixed with another basemap in one view; it shows the attribution its tiles carry, stores no tile and runs online only. Tests and continuous integration never call it; the open map stays the default and the mode of the guided story, every test and every gate.

8. **A8 — Property sale prices and bank credit** (S2-T19 for the fetch; the cost and site views of journeys D2 and D5; the forecast series of the methods). The Justice Ministry's open datasets on the national open-data portal (CC BY): the weekly real estate sales bulletin (26,719 recorded deals from 2020-07-05 to 2025-12-31 in 161 districts, as the lead's inventory of 2026-11-13 counted them) and the weekly residential units sales bulletin, and the banks' credit by economic activity. Property type and use are read from the Arabic label and the usage field and mapped in a reviewed table, because the English labels are unreliable. They give the price per square foot by district, use and quarter with deal counts and values; trends and a forecast with its backtest interval (AGENTS rule 25); the 2026 guide price of item 3 against the recorded deal price in the same district; affordability against the pay bands of residents and workers; and bank credit by sector against the sector's growth. They serve developers, hotels, banks, insurers and investors. Rents have no open source (a gap).

9. **A9 — The decision question bank** (the assistant's catalogue and evaluation sets, slices S6 to S8). The bank's own gaps (hiring and leaving flows, government tenders, rents, an events calendar, utilities and district cooling by area, supplier certifications, school capacity) wait for the owner's word and are not built now.

The owner's direction of 2026-11-13 (collect all the data first; the design of value extraction follows in a session of its own): public tenders by buying entity and category (a demand signal for business suppliers; the portal's terms are read first, [decision 0017](0017-public-data-additions-and-public-splits.md)), and district-cooling capacity by municipality and sector (open data, CC BY). 11. **The site answer's order** (journey D2, the `site_check` playbook, the retail and restaurant questions; the owner's expectation of 2026-11-13). It leads with two blocks side by side for the area picked: people working here (how many, the average and median monthly pay, the pay bands, the main sectors and employer sizes) and people living here (how many, the average and median pay, the pay bands, the age profile); then people around by hour, who passes the place shown as three separate splits by age band, by gender and by nationality group (item 16), the competitors, the costs and the rest. Each block shows the count beside the average; every figure follows document 06's release rules.

12. **The data rule** (the owner's decision of 2026-11-13, before 21:26). Only data the project is sure to have or get is used. Synthetic records are built, in the real formats and treated as real, only for the confirmed ministry sources: the employment records of private-sector individuals with their occupations and nationality group, the wages, the residence addresses (all attached to the real register companies, decision 0022), and movement (presence and movement at the grain the pack plans: by area and hour, with origin and destination, carrying the three splits of item 16). Everything else is public data held or fetched, or the company's own inputs (the files of cases C3 and C4). No synthetic data stands in for rents or tenancies, vacancy, card spending, permit or approval status, school capacity or surveys. A plot is shown only as permitted by zoning and looking empty (a parcel with no address point or building), never as approved or available. The lead's audit of the pack found six planned synthetic sources outside that list; each is replaced:
    - openings and closures of real companies by month (SYN-72, card C22, BRF-62): the register's own dates and its monthly snapshots, beside the public licence series; a snapshot of each register file is kept each month from 2026-11 beside that file, outside the repository, and is read through the same settings (`EII_REGISTER_FILE`, `EII_FC_REGISTER_FILE`);
    - invented legal entities and employer groups linking real companies (SYN-22, SYN-23): the main-and-branch structure the register shows, otherwise each registration is its own group; the privacy engine's dominance rules and their planted tests stay as they are and run on these groups;
    - the registered-address type: taken from the register files where shown, otherwise absent (as decision 0022 says);
    - the site estimate's prefilled shares and spend (card C33): the user's own inputs with no prefill, as card C35 does;
    - rents in the sensitivity runs of item 5: the user's own inputs, or the guide and recorded prices of items 3 and 8;
    - the seeded history of the oversight ledger (BE-76): the ledger starts empty and holds only questions asked; item 15 keeps it as the internal question log, with no screen.

    Attributes the confirmed sources do not plausibly hold are not generated: employment outside the private sector, deployed worksites, and trip purpose; a public anchor published by sex enters every generator other than the movement generator only as a total over the source's categories (AGENTS rule 10); the movement generator's age and gender shares are the exception, anchored on cited Public population figures, summed over the source's own categories (item 16). The sample commutes of item 1 are drawn as animated aggregate flows, never as trails of individuals (SYN-59). The road traffic of card C25 is kept as part of the movement source; the owner is asked to confirm. A register company's registration and cancellation dates are Public, and its attached records hold spells only between them, so the attacker-view model of document 06 counts both dates as attacker knowledge beside the released counts of PRV-113 item 12, with an entrant canary; this strengthens a privacy rule, as AGENTS rule 12 allows (the lead's ruling L-160 of 2026-11-14).

15. **A product for companies, designed for its personas** (the owner's decision of 2026-11-13, about 23:12, as amended by his direction of about 23:18). The product is for companies only. Every screen, story scene, label, catalogue entry and evaluation question is designed for its three personas, in this order: (a) international companies already operating in Qatar or planning to come; (b) local companies in any industry, among them real estate developers, consultants, retailers, restaurants, hotels, research bodies and universities; (c) start-ups and anyone opening a shop. Removed from version 1: the oversight seat and every ministry-only view (the oversight screen of S9-T04, the oversight read functions of S9-T01, and the oversight badge and its rules); the oversight ledger as a ministry tool; the guided story's oversight scene; any employer-against-sector view meant for a ministry; and the ministries' group of the question bank (item 9). Kept for companies: the two company seats and their separation, with the slice S9 gate tests that one company can never read another's brief or file; every privacy and release rule; the Qatarization planner as a company tool of journey D5 under the cases of item 1 (which roles Qataris hold in the private sector, at what pay, and where the talent is by district); the recovery tracker; and every company journey. The question log that the assistant's memo, replay and evaluation need stays as an internal record with no screen; it starts empty (item 12), and a planted screen that shows it fails. Scene 7 of the guided story, until now its oversight scene, becomes a company journey for the first persona: the international entrant's headquarters evidence pack. No passage names an audience of a demonstration; where the pack or the story names one, it is rewritten around the three personas, and the presenter controls stay as a feature. This narrows the build and reduces no check. It amends decision 0022 where it names the ministry view (items 3, 4, 6 and 7), decisions 0003 (item 2) and 0011 (item 4) where they name the oversight seat, and AGENTS rule 8 accordingly: a register company's other labour and residence facts appear only in analyses released under document 06 and in its own seat.

16. **Movement aggregates carry age band, gender and nationality group** (the owner's words of 2026-11-14: "gender and age group is good to have and improant for the users"; on the nationality group he left the choice to his advisor, who decided yes on 2026-11-14). The movement records (presence by area and hour; flows by origin and destination) carry three splits of the visitors of a cell: `age_band`, `gender` and `nationality_group`, as counts and shares. Each is a one-way split of the cell's total. No two splits are crossed, none is crossed with the existing `segment` dimension, and no optional path for a cross is built (decided for the owner on 2026-11-14 under his delegation). The split tables are DAT-169; their privacy rule is PRV-134; the generator's rule is SYN-89. Aggregates only: never a person-level row, and never a key that joins to a worker, residence or company record. Closed lists. `gender`: `male`, `female`. `age_band`: `15-24`, `25-34`, `35-44`, `45-54`, `55-64`, `65+`, chosen so that a public source's five-year age groups sum into them exactly; people under 15 are not carried. The band edges are held in one configuration value, so that they can be remapped to the operator's format without changing the generator or the screens (OI-148: confirm the bands when the operator's data arrives). `nationality_group`: the closed list of the worker records of decision 0022, with the same names (document 03 holds it), and one more group, `international_visitors` (visitors to the country), an aggregate like the others under the minimum-count rule. The minimum-count rule of document 06 applies to every movement cell of every split; a cell below it is withheld with its reason; a share is computed only from released counts. The three splits enter PRV-111's structural checks as breakdown dimensions of the presence and flow families. The movement generator produces the three splits inside its own aggregates. The age and gender shares are anchored on cited Public population figures, summed over the source's own categories. The nationality-group split follows the groups of the simulated people behind a cell, aggregated before anything is written; the share of international visitors is anchored on a cited Public figure of visitor arrivals. No worker, residence or company record gains an age, sex or gender attribute. An answer may show who passes a place by hour, split by age band, by gender and by nationality group (the site answer's people-here blocks of item 11, and the presence and flow cards). "Who works here" (the labour records) and "who passes here" (movement) may be shown side by side. They are never joined at the level of a person and never combined into one figure (PRV-30 holds; PRV-127 refuses a ratio across them). A split of visitors is never attributed to a named building or business (AGENTS rule 24 stands). A movement cell below the minimum count is withheld (TST-260); a movement output with a person-level row or a record key fails (TST-261); a gender attribute on a worker, residence or company table fails while the movement splits pass (TST-262).

17. **No catchment beyond 30 minutes** (the owner's words of 2026-11-14: "qatar is small 45 minutes is way too far"). No drive-time catchment anywhere in the product goes beyond 30 minutes. The closed schema refuses a request above 30 minutes (TST-256). The talent answer (card C39; item 1: the talent pool, the competing demand, the ratio of item 1 and the home-to-work flows drawn for it) uses the closed list 10, 20 and 30 minutes' drive, in place of 15, 30 and 45. Every other drive or walk area keeps 5, 10 and 15 minutes. Drive 20 and drive 30 exist only where drive 30 exists today: the staffing playbook. PRV-111's enumeration reads "drive 20 and 30 only in `staffing_and_pay`", and every list of cut-offs in the pack agrees with it.
